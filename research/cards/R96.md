## Measured (4)
- **result**: Beyond a certain batch size, especially for smaller models, inference performance plateaus indicating a shift to a compute‑bound regime. — "performance gains plateau beyond a certain batch size, especially with smaller models, a phenomenon that existing literature typically explains as a shift to the compute-bound regime." [preprint: Mind the Memory Gap: Unveiling GPU Bottlenecks in Large-Batch LLM Inference, 2025; (link refused)]
- **result**: Batching efficiency shows a sharp input‑size‑dependent transition between memory‑bound and compute‑bound regimes. — "we observe that batching efficiency exhibits a sharp input-size-dependent structure driven by the transition between memory-bound and compute-bound regimes" [paper: Razor's Edge: Throughput-Optimized Dynamic Batching with Latency Objectives, 2026; https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19360137]
- **method**: Total Kernel Launch and Queuing Time (TKLQT) can accurately identify the CPU/GPU‑bound transition point. — "We demonstrate that TKLQT accurately identifies this CPU/GPU-bound transition point" [preprint: Characterizing and Optimizing LLM Inference Workloads on CPU-GPU Coupled Architectures, 2025; (link refused)]
- **result**: There exists an optimal iteration batch size, independent of workload, that balances graph‑creation overhead with execution speedup. — "there is an optimal size of the iteration batch, which is independent of workload, balancing the extra overhead from graph creation with the performance gain of the graph execution" [paper: Boosting Performance of Iterative Applications on GPUs: Kernel Batching with CUDA Graphs, 2025; https://doi.org/10.1109/pdp66500.2025.00019]
