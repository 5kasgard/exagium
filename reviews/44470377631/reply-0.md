### Answers to the four sub‑questions (embedded in the findings)

**a) Mis‑placed or overstated gaps** – Findings 1 and 2 show that Gaps 2 and 3 are already addressed by P3 and P5 respectively; keeping them as open gaps is overstated and mis‑directs effort.

**b) Important missing gap** – Finding 4 identifies the lack of systematic cross‑hardware reproducibility and provenance, a critical omission for a project whose core claim is “real‑hardware‑calibrated cost”.

**c) Testability & pass rules** – Findings 5, 6 and 7 evaluate P21, P22 and P23: P21 and P22 are not presently testable or have unsound acceptance criteria; concrete validation suites and statistically sound ordering rules are required. The justification for P23 blocking P5 is weak (Finding 7); the blocker should be removed.

**d) Likely complexity without payoff** – Findings 8 and 9 flag the web‑view graph and the dynamic ordering algorithm as sources of scaling complexity that will consume compute and developer time without delivering proportional insight. Pruning, caching, and heuristic shortcuts are the recommended mitigations.
